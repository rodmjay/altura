A life reset retreat · Costa Rica · One week

One week to reset your life.

We lead a week-long, hands-on course to optimize every part of your life, in the idyllic highlands of Costa Rica. Money, health, home, career and retirement: you leave with a plan for each, and AI assistants keeping it on track.

Join the pilot list Pilot cohort, spring 2027. No payment to sign up.

The idea

Altura is about improving every part of your life at once, not just one. Most people run it all from memory, a calendar and a pile of tabs. In one week you write down where you want to be, then hand each part to an assistant that tracks, drafts and reminds you, wherever you are.

What you come away with

Your own team, on call.

You leave Altura with your own staff of AI assistants, each with a job title, a plan to work from and a way to reach you on your phone. The eleven below are examples of the workflows people walk away with; yours are built around your life. They do the tracking, sorting and drafting; you approve anything that spends money or speaks for you.

Runs your day

Chief of staff

A morning brief of what matters, email sorted with replies drafted, and a calendar planned around your priorities.

“Three things today: dentist at 10:30, rent is due, call Dana back.”

Keeps money on plan

Financial advisor

Every bill and due date tracked, a budget built from your real spending, and a payoff plan for debt and your credit score.

“Electric bill, $142, due Friday. Pay it from checking now? Reply 1 for yes.”

Never misses a deadline

Tax guy

Receipts, statements and forms collected as they arrive, filings and letters tracked, and one clean packet for your accountant.

“Your 1099 arrived and is filed under 2026. Two forms are still missing.”

Watches the long game

Retirement planner

Your 401(k), IRA and savings in one view, quarterly checkpoints against your goals, and the big moves modeled before you make them.

“Savings are on pace. Your move-abroad fund is 31% of the way there.”

Feeds the plan

Nutrition specialist

A meal plan for your goals and budget, a grocery cart filled for you to approve, and your health goals (weight, sleep, check-ups and lab reminders) tracked week by week.

“This week’s meals are planned. The cart is $96, four under budget.”

Keeps you moving

Personal trainer

A workout plan built around your schedule and what you’ll actually do, a nudge on days you skip, and steps, strength and weight trends week by week. It adjusts the plan when travel or a bad week gets in the way.

“You’ve hit 3 of 4 workouts. 20 minutes tonight keeps the streak. Want the hotel-room version?”

Runs the home

House manager

Groceries, cleaning and repairs on a schedule, subscriptions checked with cuts suggested, and the car, insurance and renewals tracked.

“Your registration renews in twelve days. Remind you Saturday morning?”

Grows your career

Career coach

Reads your LinkedIn messages each morning and scores every recruiter lead against what you want. For the good ones it tailors your résumé to the job description and drafts the reply, ready for you to approve and send. When an offer comes, it preps your negotiation: pay, remote terms, relocation and start date.

“Two roles match. Your résumé is tailored to each posting and replies are drafted. Send them? Reply 1 for yes.”

Gets you there

Travel agent

Fares watched on the days you like to fly, trips, credits and miles in one place, and a dated plan for living abroad.

“Salt Lake to San José dropped to $412 on a Tuesday. Hold it?”

Handles the phone

Receptionist

Texts and calls people on your behalf: confirms appointments, chases a refund, follows up on a reply. It drafts every message and only sends what you approve, and it never shares account numbers.

“The dentist can do Thursday at 10. Text back to confirm? Reply 1 for yes.”

Handles the paper

Mail clerk

Your paper mail goes to a virtual mailbox that scans it. Each day the mail clerk reads the scans, sorts them into act, keep and junk, sends bills to your financial advisor and files the rest.

“Three pieces today: a water bill for $48, a jury summons, two catalogues. Bill sent to your financial advisor.”

      Access and comfort

      What the week asks of you, plainly.

      Before you decide, here is what each part of the week asks of your body and senses, and the other way to take part in it. You never have to explain why you need something.

      Getting there
      What it asks

      A flight to SJO, then about 25 minutes by van on winding hill roads.

      The other way

      The front seat, a stop whenever you need one, and help with bags from the kerb to your room.

      Height and air
      What it asks

      The hills above Alajuela sit at roughly 900 to 1,200 metres. Poás crater is about 2,700 metres, cool and sometimes smells of sulphur.

      The other way

      Wednesday is a rest day, so the volcano is optional. Hot springs, the pool or a slow morning instead.

      Walking
      What it asks

      The sample waterfall hike has wet stone paths and stairs. At Poás, a paved path of under a kilometre climbs gently to the viewpoint.

      The other way

      A short, level garden route, transport to the nearest point, or the afternoon at the base with build time and a coach.

      Sessions
      What it asks

      Two classes a day at a laptop, three hours in the morning and two in the late afternoon, with talking in small groups.

      The other way

      The agenda and slides a week ahead, written notes after each session, breaks whenever you like and a quiet room to build alone.

      Evenings
      What it asks

      Shared dinners with open seating. Some nights get lively and loud.

      The other way

      A quieter table, leaving whenever you want with nothing to explain, or dinner in your room.

      Food and climate
      What it asks

      Most meals are shared from one kitchen. Days are warm, around 24 to 28°C, with strong sun at altitude; May brings afternoon rain.

      The other way

      Every dietary need or allergy asked about before you fly, with dishes labelled. Shade, water and rest are always close.
